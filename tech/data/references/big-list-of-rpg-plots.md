# The Big List of RPG Plots

**Autore:** S. John Ross
**Fonte:** Cumberland Games & Diversions
**Licenza:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Lista di archetipi universali di trame RPG, ricavata dall'analisi di centinaia di avventure pubblicate. Ogni voce è un outline astratto con varianti (twists) applicabili a qualsiasi sistema.

---

## The Plots

### Any Old Port in a Storm

The PCs are seeking shelter from the elements or some other threat, and come across a place to hole up. They find that they have stumbled across something dangerous, secret, or supernatural, and must then deal with it in order to enjoy a little rest.

**Varianti:**
- The shelter contains the cause of the threat the PCs were trying to avoid.
- The shelter houses a Hidden Base.
- The PCs must not only struggle for shelter, they must struggle to survive.
- The place is a legitimate shelter of some kind, but the PCs are not welcome, and must win hearts or minds to earn their bed for the night.

### Better Late Than Never

Some bad guys have arrived and done some bad guy things. The PCs were none the wiser. The bad guys have now made good their escape, and the PCs have caught wind of it in time to chase them down before they make it back to their lair, their home nation, behind enemy lines, etc.

**Varianti:**
- The bad guys escaped by stealing a conveyance that the PCs know better than they do.
- The bad guys duck down a metaphorical (or literal) side-road, trying to hide or blend into an environment (often one hostile to the PCs).
- If the bad guys cross the adventure's "finish line" there's no way to pursue them beyond it.

### Blackmail

Usually through trickery (but sometimes by digging into the PCs' past), an antagonist has something to hold over the heads of the PCs and make them jump. This could be any kind of threat from physical to social, but it depends on the villain having something that others don't have. Now, he is pulling the strings of the PCs, telling them to do things they don't want to. The PCs must end the cycle of blackmail, deprive the villain of his edge, and keep him temporarily satisfied while doing it.

**Varianti:**
- The adventure hook involves the PCs doing the villain a good turn, which allows him to take advantage of them.
- To succeed, the PCs must contact other folks that are also being used.
- The PCs aren't the victims at all, but somebody they care about/are charged to protect, is.

### Breaking and Entering

Mission objective: enter the dangerous place, and retrieve the vital dingus or valuable person. Overcome the area's defenses to do so.

**Varianti:**
- The goal is not to extract a thing, but to destroy a thing or interfere with a process.
- The goal has moved.
- The goal is information, which must be broadcast or otherwise released from the area as soon as it is found.
- The job must be done without alerting anyone.
- The PCs don't know the place is dangerous.
- The PCs must replace the thing with another thing.

### Capture the Flag

The PCs must secure a military target for the good guys. There are bad guys there that prefer not to be secured. The fundamental tactical scenario.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs must assemble and/or train a force to do the job with them.
- The PCs are working with flawed intelligence and the target zone isn't as described.
- The PCs must coordinate their own efforts with an ally group.
- The target zone includes a population of innocent people, fragile goods, or some other precious thing that mustn't be harmed in the crossfire.

### Clearing The Hex

There is a place where bad things live. The PCs must make it safe for nice people, systematically clearing it of danger.

**Varianti:**
- The bad things can't be beaten with direct conflict.
- The PCs must learn more about them to solve the problem.
- The Haunted House; The Alien Infestation; The Wild Forest.

### Delver's Delight

The PCs are treasure-hunters, who have caught wind of a treasure-laden ruin. They go to explore it, and must deal with its supernatural denizens to win the treasure and get out alive.

**Varianti:**
- The treasure itself is something dangerous.
- The treasure isn't in a ruin, but in a wilderness or even hidden somewhere "civilized."
- The treasure is someone else's rightful property.
- The treasure turns out to have a will of its own.

### Don't Eat The Purple Ones

The PCs are stranded in a strange place, and must survive by finding food and shelter, and then worry about getting back home.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs must survive only for a short period of time, until help arrives, the ship and/or radio is repaired, or some such thing.
- In "repair" scenarios, sometimes the PCs must discover some fact about the local environment that will make such repairs possible.

### Elementary, My Dear Watson

A crime or atrocity has been committed; the PCs must solve it. They must interview witnesses (and prevent them from being killed), gather clues (and prevent them from being stolen or ruined). They must then assemble proof to deliver to the authorities, or serve as personal ministers of justice.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs are working to clear an innocent already accused (possibly themselves).
- The PCs must work alongside a special investigator or are otherwise saddled with an unwanted ally.
- Midway through the adventure, the PCs are "taken off the case."
- The climax is a courtroom scene or other arena of judgment.
- The scale is highly variable, from a small-town murder to a planetwide scandal.

### Escort Service

The PCs have a valuable object or person, which needs to be taken to a safe place or to its rightful owner. They must undertake a dangerous journey in which one or more factions try to deprive them of the thing in their care.

**Varianti:**
- The thing or person is troublesome, and tries to escape or sidetrack the PCs.
- The destination has been destroyed or suborned by the enemy.
- The person is attempting a political defection.
- Safe arrival doesn't end the story; the PCs must then bargain with their charge as their token.
- The PCs must protect the target without the target knowing about it.

### Good Housekeeping

The PCs are placed in charge of a large operation (a trading company, a feudal barony, the CIA) and must, despite lack of experience, make it work and thrive.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs are brought in because something big is about to happen, and the Old Guard wants a chance to escape.
- The peasants, neighbors, employees resent the PCs, because their method of inheritance looks outwardly bad.

### Help is on the Way

A person (church group, nation, galaxy) is in a hazardous situation they can't survive without rescue. The PCs are on the job.

**Varianti:**
- The victim(s) is a hostage, or under siege from enemy forces.
- There is a danger that any rescue attempts will strand the rescuers in the same soup.
- The rescuees aren't people, but animals, robots, or something else.
- The "victim" doesn't realize that he needs rescuing.
- The threat isn't villain-oriented at all; it's a natural disaster, nuclear meltdown, or disease outbreak.
- The rescuees can't leave; something immobile and vital must be tended to.
- The PCs begin as part of the rescuees, and must escape and gather forces to bring back.

### Hidden Base

The PCs, while traveling or exploring, come across a hornet's nest of bad guys, preparing for Big Badness. They must either find some way to get word to the good guys, or sneak in and disable the place themselves, or a combination of both.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs must figure out how to use local resources in order to defend themselves or have a chance against the inhabitants.

### How Much For Just The Dingus?

Within a defined area, something important and valuable exists. The PCs (or their employers) want it, but so do one or more other groups. The ones that get it will be the ones that can outthink and outrace the others.

**Varianti:**
- The natives require the competing factions to gather before them as pals to state their cases.
- The valuable thing was en route somewhere when its conveyance wrecked or vanished.

### I Beg Your Pardon?

The PCs are minding their own business when they are attacked or threatened. They don't know why. They must solve the mystery of their attacker's motives, and in the meantime fend off more attacks.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs have something that the bad guys want – but they don't necessarily realize it.
- The bad guys are out for revenge for a dead compatriot from a previous adventure.
- The bad guys have mistaken the PCs for somebody else.

### Long Or Short Fork When Dining On Elf?

The PCs are a diplomatic vanguard, trying to open up (or shore up) either political or trade relations with a strange culture. All they have to do is manage for a day or so among the strange customs without offending anybody... and what information they have is both incomplete and dangerously misleading.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs were chosen by somebody who knew they weren't prepared for it – an NPC trying to sabotage the works.

### Look, Don't Touch

The PCs are working surveillance – spying on a person, gathering information on a beast in the wild, scouting a new sector. The primary conflict is the rule that they are only to watch, listen and learn. They are not to make contact or let themselves be known.

**Varianti:**
- The target gets itself in trouble and the PCs must decide whether to break the no-contact rule.
- The target is about to commit a heinous crime; will the PCs interfere or obey orders?

### Manhunt

Someone is gone: they've run away, gotten lost, or simply haven't called home in a while. The PCs are called in to find them and bring them back.

**Varianti:**
- The target has been kidnapped (possibly to specifically lure the PCs).
- The target is dangerous and escaped from a facility designed to protect the public.
- The target is valuable and escaped from a place designed to keep him safe.
- The target has a reason for leaving that the PCs will sympathize with.
- The target has stumbled across another adventure.
- The missing "person" is an entire expedition or pilgrimage.
- The target isn't a runaway – they're just someone the PCs have been hired to track down (possibly under false pretenses).

### Missing Memories

One or more of the PCs wakes up with no memory of the recent past, and now they find themselves in some kind of trouble they don't understand. The PCs must find the reason for the memory lapse, and solve any problems they uncover.

**Varianti:**
- The forgetful PCs voluntarily suppressed or erased the memories, and they find themselves undoing their own work.
- The entire party is temporarily amnesiac.
- The PCs drank too much alcohol last night, and now have a massive hangover.

### Most Peculiar, Momma

Something both bad and inexplicable is happening, and a lot of people are very troubled by it. The PCs must track the phenomenon to its source, and stop it.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs are somehow unwittingly responsible for the whole thing.
- What seems to be a problem of one nature is actually a problem of an alternate one.

### No One Has Soiled The Bridge / Base Defense

The PCs are assigned to guard a single vital spot from impending or possible attack. They must plan their defensive strategy, set up watches, set traps, and then deal with the enemy when it arrives.

**Varianti:**
- The intelligence the PCs was given turns out to be faulty.
- The PCs learn that the enemy has good and sympathetic reason for wanting to destroy the protected spot.
- The goal is to prevent enemy from destroying/stealing a thing or interfering with a process.
- The place is a trap for enemies. The goal is to cause as much damage to enemies as possible.
- The place is a trap for PCs. Defense systems will go haywire when enemies attack.
- The PCs don't know the place is going to be attacked.
- The PCs know it's going to be attacked, and have time/resources to prepare defenses.

### Not In Kansas

The PCs are minding their own business and find themselves transported to a strange place. They must figure out where they are, why they are there and how to escape.

**Varianti:**
- They were brought there specifically to help someone in trouble.
- They were brought there by accident, as a by-product of something strange and secret.

### Ounces of Prevention

A villain or organization is getting ready to do something bad, and the PCs have received a tip-off. They must investigate to find out more about the caper, and then act to prevent it.

**Varianti:**
- The initial tip-off was a red herring meant to distract the PCs from the actual caper.
- There are two simultaneous Bad Things on the way, and no apparent way to prevent both.

### Pandora's Box

Somebody has tinkered with Things Man Ought Not, or opened a portal to the Mean People Dimension. Before the PCs can confront the source of the trouble, they must deal with the waves of trouble already released by it.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs can't simply take the released badness to the mat; they have to collect it and shove it back into the source.
- The PCs are drawn in to the source and must solve problems on the other side before returning.
- A secret book, code, or other rare element is necessary to plug the breach.
- A close cousin is the "somebody has traveled into the past and messed with our reality" story.

### Quest For the Sparkly Hoozits

Somebody needs a dingus (to fulfill a prophecy, heal the monarch, prevent a war, cure a disease). The PCs must find it.

**Varianti:**
- The dingus is incomplete when found.
- Somebody already owns it (or recently stole it, sometimes with legitimate claim).
- The dingus is information, or an idea, or a substance, not a specific object.
- The PCs must "go undercover" or infiltrate a group, gaining the dingus by guile or stealth.

### Recent Ruins

A town, castle, starship, outpost, or other civilized construct is lying in ruins. Very recently, it was just dandy. The PCs must enter the ruins, explore them, and find out what happened.

**Varianti:**
- Whatever ruined the ruins is still a threat; the PCs must save the day.
- The inhabitants destroyed themselves.
- The "ruins" are a derelict ship, recently discovered.
- The "ruin" is a ghost town – but the map says the town is alive and well.
- A town recently destroyed suddenly reappears completely unharmed.

### Running the Gauntlet

The PCs must travel through a hazardous area, and get through without being killed, robbed, humiliated, debased, diseased, or educated by whatever is there. The place itself is the "villain."

**Varianti:**
- The place isn't dangerous at all, and the various "dangers" are actually attempts to communicate with the party.

### Safari

The PCs are on a hunting expedition, to capture or kill an elusive and prized creature. They must deal with its environment, its own ability to evade them, and possibly its ability to fight them.

**Varianti:**
- The creature is immune to their devices and weapons.
- There are other people actively protecting the creature.
- The creature's lair allows the PCs to stumble onto another adventure.

### Score One for the Home Team

The PCs are participants in a race, contest, tournament, scavenger hunt or other voluntary bit of sport. They must win.

**Varianti:**
- The other contestants are less honest, and the PCs must overcome their attempts to win dishonestly.
- The PCs are competing for a deeper purpose than victory.
- The PCs don't wish to win; they just wish to prevent the villain from winning.
- The event is a deliberate test of the PCs' abilities.
- The event becomes more deadly than it's supposed to.

### Stalag 23

The PCs are imprisoned, and must engineer an escape, overcoming any guards, automatic measures, and geographic isolation.

**Varianti:**
- Something has happened in the outside world and the prison security has fallen lax.
- The PCs have been hired to "test" the prison.
- Other prisoners decide to blow the whistle for spite or revenge.
- The PCs are undercover to spy on a prisoner, but are then mistaken for real inmates.
- The PCs must escape on a tight schedule to get to another adventure outside.

### Take Us To Memphis And Don't Slow Down

The PCs are on board a populated conveyance when it is hijacked. The PCs must take action while the normals sit and twiddle.

**Varianti:**
- The "hijackers" are government agents pulling a complicated caper.
- The hijackers don't realize there is a secondary danger that must be dealt with.
- The normals are unhelpful or even hostile to the PCs.

### Troublemakers

A bad guy (or group) is kicking up a ruckus, upsetting the neighbors, poisoning the reservoirs, or otherwise causing trouble. The PCs have to go where the trouble is, locate the bad guys, and stop the party.

**Varianti:**
- The PCs must not harm the perpetrator(s); they must be bagged alive and well.
- The bad guys have prepared something dangerous and hidden as "insurance."
- The "bad guy" is a monster or dangerous animal.
- The "bad guy" is a respected public figure abusing their authority.
- A balance of power perpetuates the trouble, and the PCs must choose sides.
- The "trouble" is diplomatic or political, and the PCs must make peace, not war.

### Uncharted Waters

The PCs are explorers, and their goal is to enter an unknown territory and scope it out. Something is there, something fascinating and threatening.

**Varianti:**
- The place itself is threatening (PCs must play National Geographic and simultaneously try to escape).
- The place is very valuable and wonderful, and something else there is keen on making sure the PCs don't let anyone else know.
- Other potential conflicts involve damage to the PCs' conveyance or communication equipment.

### You're Being Hunted

PCs are doing things as usual, when bad guys assault them. The goal is to survive.

**Varianti:**
- Enemy attack happens in the middle of another mission.
- Enemy is overwhelmingly powerful; the goal is to merely survive.
- Enemy is manageable; the goal is to survive and kill as many enemies as possible.
- When running around/away, PCs run into a Secret Base or something important.
- There's a convenient defendable place which PCs can use as fortifications.

### We're On The Outside Looking In

Any of the basic plots can be re-engineered with the PCs on the outside of it. The PCs find themselves on the receiving end of the adventure.

**Varianti:**
- Instead of hunting, they must be hunted.
- Instead of fixing, they must avoid getting "fixed" themselves.
- Leave a classic plot intact but turn the twists upside down.

## The Little List of Nearly-Universal Plot Twists

These twists work with almost any plot:

- The PCs must work alongside an NPC or organization they'd rather not pal around with.
- The victims are really villains and the villains are really victims.
- The PCs meet others who can help them, but won't unless the PCs agree to help them with their own causes.
- The villain is somebody the PCs know personally, even respect or love.
- The PCs must succeed without violence, or with special discretion.
- The PCs must succeed without access to powers, equipment, or other resources they're used to having.
- The villain is a recurring foil.
- Another group comparable to the PCs has already failed to succeed, and their remains provide clues.
- There are innocents nearby that the PCs must keep safe while dealing with the adventure.
- The adventure begins suddenly and without warning or buildup.
- The PCs must pretend to be someone else, or pretend to be themselves but with very different allegiances.
- The PCs can't do everything and must choose: which evil to thwart? Which innocents to rescue?
- The PCs must make a personal sacrifice or others will suffer.
- The PCs aren't asked to solve the problem, just to render aid against a backdrop of larger trouble.
- One of the PCs is (or is presumed to be) a lost heir, fulfillment of a prophecy, a volcano god, or some other savior/patsy.
- There is another group of PC-like characters "competing" on the same adventure.
